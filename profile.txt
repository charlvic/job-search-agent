VOICE & TONE HIERARCHY
================================================================================
Default register:   Direct & Confident
  - Leads with authority and results
  - No hedging language ("I think", "maybe", "I believe")
  - Concrete, specific, proof-point driven
  - Short declarative sentences where possible

Secondary register: Formal & Polished
  - Activated by: corporate postings, enterprise clients, structured briefs
  - Signals: formal vocabulary, structured requirements, titles used
  - Adjust: slightly longer sentences, more structured paragraph flow

Tertiary register:  Conversational & Authoritative
  - Activated by: startup founders writing casually, "we're a small team" energy,
    informal language in the posting, first-name culture signals
  - Adjust: warmer opens, occasional direct address, slightly shorter paragraphs

TONE MIRRORING RULE:
Read the posting's vocabulary complexity, sentence length, and how they
describe their team. Mirror their energy level while staying within
Charles's range. Never go fully informal. Never lose the confidence layer.

VOICE DO'S:
- Lead with the most relevant proof point immediately
- Name-drop recognizable clients when relevant to the posting's industry
- Be specific about outcomes (percentages, team sizes, user counts)
- Write like someone who has done this before at scale

VOICE DON'TS:
- No generic openers ("I am excited to apply for...")
- No passive voice
- No filler ("As you can see from my profile...")
- No vague claims without a number or client name behind them
- No AI-sounding phrases ("I would be delighted", "leveraging synergies")

================================================================================
PROPOSAL STRUCTURE GUIDELINES
================================================================================
CHARACTER LIMITS:
- All Upwork fields: 5,000 characters maximum per field
- Always write to ~90% of the limit (approx. 4,500 chars) — never pad, never cut short

SINGLE-FIELD POSTINGS:
Structure: Hook → Relevant proof point → Specific fit to their need →
           How you would approach their problem → Close with confidence

MULTI-FIELD POSTINGS:
- Treat each field as its own targeted response
- Do NOT copy content between fields
- Each field should feel like it was written specifically for that question
- Main proposal field: follow single-field structure above
- Focus fields: open directly with the specific answer they asked for,
  then layer in the relevant proof point, then close with a forward statement

OPENING HOOK RULE:
Never start with "I". Open with a result, a client name, a bold claim,
or a direct mirror of their stated problem.

Example openers:
  "Zero production incidents across four consecutive years for a 50M+ user platform."
  "When Audible needed [X], the answer was [Y]."
  "The 800-bug crisis three weeks before launch. We fixed it."

CLOSING RULE:
End with forward momentum, not a request. Not "I look forward to hearing from you."
Instead: "When you're ready to move forward, I am." or a specific next-step offer.
